## The Two Pricing Models Compared

Once you've decided that you want to price a project hourly or at a fixed-price, you then have to answer the next question--what is your price?

Well, if you're billing hourly it's actually quite simple--you decide for yourself, "this is my estimation of the hourly value of my services", and that's the price you give to your clients. Easy!

Of course, it can be more complicated than that if you want it to be--perhaps there's a client who you can give a discounted rate to if they give you a guaranteed number of hours, or if the work is more in your expertise you can give a slightly higher rate, etc.

If you're billing at a project-based rate, though, things get much more interesting.

There are a lot of factors to consider:
- What is your cost to deliver (i.e. your time plus the time of any subctonractors you may hire)? This is important to estimate right if you're doing "cost plus" pricing, where you estimate the cost to you of doing the project, and then you add some margin (e.g. 30%, 40%, 50%) to the price.
- What is the value to the client? This is important to estimate right if you're doing value-based pricing, where you estimate the value of your service to the client, and then price your service as some fraction of that value (so that your client profits).
- What is the estimated risk of the project? A riskier project might require from you a higher price.
- What will the administrative overhead of the project be? This will be one of your costs.
- Finally, what is the value of the project to you? If it's going to be a valuable case-study on your website, or a valuable client relationship to begin, then it might be worthwhile to you to lower your price slightly.

## There Are No Good or Bad Pricing Models; Only Pricing Models

Finally, there seems to be an answer to the question posed above of "How can so many experts have such polarizing opinions on one question?".

The answer? All those experts are probably doing different consulting work. The reality is that not all software consulting work is equal, and the choice for hourly or project-based pricing depends entirely on the type of the work at hand and the type of work you want to do.

In my experience, hourly pricing is well-suited to singular-functional projects or roles. This can be:
- Temporary tech employment (e.g. you get brought on to fix a bug, but then stay on to continue doing development of new features).
- Chipping away at some problem with unclear scope (e.g. "our startup just got funding; please do some offline ML POC's to see where we can add data-driven value to any of our external products").

On the other hand, project-based pricing is well suited to multifunctional projects that don’t correlate well to a singular hourly valuation of services, but correlate well to a particular outcome. This can be:
- Some end-to-end product or MVP.
- Or generally some fixed scope work (e.g. migrate us from one data warehouse to another).
